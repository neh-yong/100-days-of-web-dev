# What Is Web3? (+ How Does It Work?)

## 1. What is Web3?

**Web3 (or Web3.0)** refers to an idea for the next stage of internet development.

It aims to create a more **decentralized internet** where users have greater control and ownership over their:

* Data
* Digital assets
* Online identity

Web3 is built around **blockchain technology**, reducing dependence on centralized companies and platforms.

The term **Web3** was introduced in **2014 by Gavin Wood**, a co-founder of Ethereum.

### Main Idea

**Web 2.0:** Companies/platforms control  much of the infrastructure and user data.

**Web3:** Users are intended to have greater control over their data and digital assets.

---

# 2. What is a Web3 Wallet?

A **Web3 wallet** is a digital tool that:

* Stores private keys.
* Allows users to interact with blockchain networks.
* Manages cryptocurrencies.
* Connects users to decentralized applications (**dApps**).

Unlike traditional financial wallets, users maintain direct control over their assets rather than relying on banks or centralized platforms.

---

# 3. How Do Companies Monetize Our Data?

In **Web 2.0**, companies collect information about users' online activities.

For example, when you browse websites or use social media, platforms can collect information about your interests and behavior.

Companies can then use this data to provide targeted advertising and monetize it by selling advertising opportunities to businesses.

---

# 4. Web3 vs. Metaverse

**Web3 and the metaverse are not the same thing.**

### Web3

Describes a possible way for the internet to evolve toward greater decentralization, user ownership, and control.

### Metaverse

Refers to an immersive virtual environment where people interact with each other and digital spaces.

The metaverse could operate under:

* A centralized organization — similar to the Web 2.0 model.
* A peer-to-peer network — following Web3 principles.

### Simple Difference

> **Web3 = how the internet's underlying structure can evolve.**
> **Metaverse = an immersive virtual environment.**

# 5. History of the Web

To understand Web3, it helps to understand how the web evolved.

## Web 1.0 — Read

Web 1.0 emerged in the early 1990s.

Characteristics:

* Mostly static web pages.
* HTML-based websites.
* Users primarily consumed information.
* The web was relatively open and decentralized.

Examples included websites such as WebMD and GeoCities.

### Mental Model

**Web 1.0 → Read**

---

## Web 2.0 — Read + Write

Web 2.0 became prominent in the early-to-mid 2000s.

The internet became much more interactive through:

* Blogs
* Forums
* Wikis
* Social media
* User-generated content

However, large technology companies gained significant control over internet infrastructure and user data.

Examples mentioned in the article include:

* Meta
* Twitter
* Google
* Amazon

### Mental Model

**Web 2.0 → Read + Write**

---

## Web3 — Read + Write + Ownership

Web3 aims to combine:

* The decentralization associated with Web 1.0
* The interactivity of Web 2.0
* Greater user ownership and control

### Mental Model

**Web3 → Read + Write + Ownership**

# 6. How Does Web3 Work?

Web3 works by using **decentralized networks**, particularly blockchain technology.

Instead of relying entirely on centralized servers controlled by large companies, information can be distributed across a network.

### Blockchain's Role

Blockchain acts as a decentralized ledger that:

* Stores data in blocks.
* Records transactions and changes.
* Allows network participants to see the recorded information.
* Makes records difficult to alter.

Web3 can therefore provide users with more control over their online activities and digital assets.

Users participating in some Web3 ecosystems may also receive **tokens** as incentives for contributing to the development of the network.

---

# 7. Advantages of Web3

Many proposed advantages of Web3 come from its decentralized structure.

### 1. Interaction

Users can potentially have more opportunities to interact with content and other users.

### 2. Ownership

Users can become participants and owners within communities rather than simply being consumers.

They may receive incentives for participating instead of primarily exchanging personal data for access to platforms.

### 3. Permissions

Users can have access to network data and services without depending on a single centralized platform.

### 4. Privacy

In the article's model, a user's identity is associated with their **digital wallet**.

Online activity can be publicly visible while the user's real-world identity can remain hidden.

### 5. Speed

The article notes that technologies such as:

* Artificial intelligence
* Machine learning
* Smart contracts
* Blockchain

can help provide relevant data and services more efficiently.

---

# 8. Disadvantages of Web3

Web3 also has important limitations.

### 1. Cost

Blockchain technology can be expensive and energy-intensive, which can increase the cost of Web3 systems.

### 2. Hardware Requirements

Users may need newer or more capable hardware to take full advantage of some Web3 applications.

### 3. Regulation

Decentralization shifts some responsibility for regulating content and user actions toward the community.

This can make moderation and governance more difficult.

### 4. Scalability

Blockchain-based peer-to-peer systems can require significant computing resources.

Current scalability limitations can:

* Increase costs.
* Restrict participation.
* Potentially lead to centralization.

# 9. How to Get Into Web3

Web3 provides opportunities across both technical and non-technical roles.

## Web3 Career Examples

### Beta Tester

Tests new Web3 applications and reports problems to developers.

### Community Manager / Moderator

Manages online communities, helps onboard members, maintains resources, and resolves community issues.

### Cryptocurrency / NFT Researcher or Analyst

Studies cryptocurrency, digital assets, and NFTs, including their market performance and pricing.

### Marketer

Promotes products and services while focusing on community building, relationships, and engagement.

### Technical Writer

Explains complex Web3 concepts in simpler language for users and audiences.

### Web3 Developer

Can work in:

* Front-end development
* Back-end development
* Full-stack development

Tasks can include writing and testing code, designing interfaces, and building smart contracts.

# 11. Key Takeaways

* **Web3** is an idea for the next evolution of the internet.
* It aims to provide greater **decentralization, user ownership, privacy, and control**.
* Web3 was introduced as a term by **Gavin Wood in 2014**.
* **Blockchain technology** is a fundamental technology behind Web3.
* A **Web3 wallet** allows users to manage private keys, digital assets, and interact with dApps.
* **Web 1.0** was primarily about consuming information.
* **Web 2.0** introduced large-scale interaction and user-generated content but increased the power of centralized platforms.
* **Web3** aims to combine decentralization with interactivity and user ownership.
* Web3 has potential benefits such as interaction, ownership, permissions, privacy, and speed.
* Major challenges include **cost, hardware requirements, regulation, and scalability**.
* Web3 offers both technical and non-technical career opportunities.
* Important technical areas include **blockchain, smart contracts, Solidity, EVM, programming, and data structures**.
