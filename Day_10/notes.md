# What is Cryptocurrency and How Does It Work?

> **Source:** Kaspersky — *What is Cryptocurrency and how does it work?*

## 1. What is Cryptocurrency?

**Cryptocurrency** is a form of currency that exists digitally or virtually and uses **cryptography** to secure transactions.

Unlike traditional currencies, cryptocurrencies:

* Do not have a central issuing or regulating authority.
* Use a **decentralized system** to record transactions and issue new units.
* Allow people to send and receive payments through a **peer-to-peer (P2P)** system.
* Exist as digital records rather than physical money.
* Store cryptocurrency through **digital wallets**.

### Why is it called Cryptocurrency?

The name comes from **cryptography**, which is used to secure and verify transactions.

Encryption helps protect cryptocurrency data when it is stored, transmitted between wallets, and recorded on public ledgers.

---

## 2. How Does Cryptocurrency Work?

Cryptocurrencies operate using a **distributed public ledger called a blockchain**.

The blockchain:

* Records cryptocurrency transactions.
* Is distributed among currency holders.
* Is continuously updated with new transactions.
* Makes the transaction history difficult to tamper with.

### How are new cryptocurrency units created?

New units can be created through **mining**.

**Mining** involves using computer processing power to solve complicated mathematical problems. Successfully completing these problems can generate new cryptocurrency units.

Cryptocurrency can also be:

1. Purchased through brokers or exchanges.
2. Stored in cryptographic wallets.
3. Sent to other users.

### What do you actually own?

When you own cryptocurrency, you don't physically own a coin or other tangible object.

Instead, you own a **key** that allows you to move a record or unit of cryptocurrency from one person to another without requiring a trusted third party.

## 3. First Cryptocurrency

The first cryptocurrency was **Bitcoin**, founded in **2009**.

* Bitcoin remains the best-known cryptocurrency.
* It was developed by **Satoshi Nakamoto**.
* Satoshi Nakamoto is widely believed to be a pseudonym for an individual or group.
* Their precise identity remains unknown.

---

## 4. Examples of Cryptocurrencies

There are thousands of cryptocurrencies.

### Bitcoin (BTC)

* Founded in 2009.
* First cryptocurrency.
* Still the most commonly traded.
* Developed by Satoshi Nakamoto.

### Ethereum (ETH)

* Developed in 2015.
* Ethereum is a blockchain platform.
* **Ether (ETH)** is its native cryptocurrency.
* It is one of the most popular cryptocurrencies after Bitcoin.

### Litecoin (LTC)

* Similar to Bitcoin.
* Focused on faster payments and processes that allow more transactions.

### Ripple

* Distributed ledger system founded in 2012.
* Can track different types of transactions, not just cryptocurrency.
* Its associated company has worked with banks and financial institutions.

### Altcoins

**Altcoins** is the collective term for cryptocurrencies other than Bitcoin.

## 5. How to Buy Cryptocurrency

The article describes three main steps.

### Step 1: Choose a Platform

There are two main choices:

**Traditional brokers**

* Allow users to buy and sell cryptocurrency along with assets such as stocks, bonds, and ETFs.
* Tend to have lower trading costs.
* Usually offer fewer crypto-specific features.

**Cryptocurrency exchanges**

* Specifically provide cryptocurrency trading and related services.
* May offer different cryptocurrencies, wallet storage, and interest-bearing accounts.
* Often charge asset-based fees.

When comparing platforms, consider:

* Available cryptocurrencies
* Trading fees
* Security features
* Storage options
* Withdrawal options
* Educational resources

### Step 2: Fund Your Account

Most exchanges allow users to purchase cryptocurrency using **fiat currency**, such as:

* US Dollar (USD)
* British Pound (GBP)
* Euro (EUR)

Possible payment methods include:

* Debit/credit cards
* ACH transfers
* Wire transfers

Fees may apply to:

* Deposits
* Withdrawals
* Trading

Credit-card purchases can be risky because cryptocurrency prices are highly volatile and some providers do not support crypto transactions.

### Step 3: Place an Order

Through a broker or exchange:

1. Select **Buy** or **Sell**.
2. Choose the cryptocurrency.
3. Select the order type.
4. Enter the amount.
5. Confirm the order.

## 7. How to Store Cryptocurrency

Cryptocurrency is generally stored using **crypto wallets**.

A wallet is used to securely manage the **private keys** associated with cryptocurrency.

There are two major types:

### Hot Wallet

* Uses online software.
* Connected to the internet.
* Used to protect and manage private keys.
* Typically does not charge fees according to the article.

### Cold Wallet

* Also called a **hardware wallet**.
* Uses an offline electronic device.
* Keeps private keys offline.
* Typically charges fees.

**Key idea:**

> Hot wallet = online
> Cold wallet = offline

## 9. Cryptocurrency Scams and Fraud

Cryptocurrency crime and scams are significant risks.

Common scams include:

### Fake Websites

Fraudulent websites promise massive or guaranteed returns to convince people to invest.

### Virtual Ponzi Schemes

Criminals create fake investment opportunities and use money from new investors to pay earlier investors, creating the illusion of profitability.

### Fake Celebrity Endorsements

Scammers impersonate celebrities or influential people and encourage users to invest in a cryptocurrency.

### Romance Scams

Scammers build relationships with victims through dating apps or social media and eventually persuade them to invest in cryptocurrency.

### Fake Exchanges and Traders

Fraudsters pretend to be legitimate cryptocurrency traders or create fake exchanges to steal users' money.

### Cryptocurrency Hacking

Attackers can target digital wallets and steal cryptocurrency.

## 10. Is Cryptocurrency Safe?

Cryptocurrencies commonly use **blockchain technology** to record transactions in blocks and timestamp them.

This creates a digital transaction ledger that is difficult to tamper with.

However:

> **Difficult to tamper with does NOT mean impossible to hack.**

Cryptocurrency systems and companies have experienced major hacks.

Another major risk is **price volatility**.

Unlike government-backed currencies, cryptocurrency values are largely driven by **supply and demand**, which can cause dramatic price changes.

Therefore:

* Prices can rise significantly.
* Prices can also fall significantly.
* Cryptocurrency generally has less regulatory protection than traditional financial products such as stocks, bonds, and mutual funds.

## 11. Four Tips for Safer Crypto Investing

### 1. Research Exchanges

Before investing:

* Research cryptocurrency exchanges.
* Read reviews.
* Learn from experienced investors.
* Understand the exchange you are using.

### 2. Understand How to Store Your Crypto

Decide whether to:

* Keep cryptocurrency on an exchange.
* Use a hot wallet.
* Use a cold/hardware wallet.

Understand the security and technical requirements of your chosen storage method.

### 3. Diversify Your Investments

Don't put all your money into a single cryptocurrency.

Diversification can help reduce the risk associated with depending entirely on one asset.

### 4. Prepare for Volatility

Cryptocurrency markets can experience dramatic price fluctuations.

You should understand and be prepared for the possibility of significant losses.

# Key Takeaways

* **Cryptocurrency** is digital/virtual money secured using cryptography.
* It generally operates without a central issuing authority.
* **Blockchain** acts as a distributed public ledger for cryptocurrency transactions.
* **Mining** can be used to create new cryptocurrency units.
* **Bitcoin** was the first cryptocurrency, launched in 2009.
* **Altcoins** are cryptocurrencies other than Bitcoin.
* Cryptocurrency can be bought through **brokers and exchanges**.
* **Wallets** are used to manage the private keys associated with cryptocurrency.
* **Hot wallets** are online, while **cold wallets** are offline.
* Cryptocurrency can be used to purchase certain goods and services.
* Crypto carries risks including **scams, hacking, and extreme price volatility**.
* Cryptocurrency is still a relatively new and speculative technology.
* Research, secure storage, diversification, and understanding volatility are important when dealing with crypto.
